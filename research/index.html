Research

Our work spans three connected questions: how the caudal nervous system self-organizes, how that process is disrupted in disease, and how either can be measured with sufficient rigor to support preclinical decisions.

01

Self-organization of the caudal neural tube

Neurulation · Neuromesodermal progenitors · Axis patterning

The vertebrate nervous system arises from the neural tube, which forms through neurulation and elongates caudally from a population of neuromesodermal progenitors at the tail bud. Positional identity along this axis is assigned by opposing WNT and retinoic acid gradients, which we have shown also govern the adhesion and collective migration properties of caudal neural progenitors during axial elongation.

We reconstruct this process in vitro. Under geometric confinement, human pluripotent stem cells break symmetry and undergo caudalization, generating spinal cord organoids that recapitulate neural tube morphogenesis: lumen formation, apicobasal polarity, and self-ordered dorsoventral patterning established through the spontaneous expression of patterning morphogens rather than exogenous instruction.

02

Modeling developmental and degenerative disorders

Neural tube defects · Developmental neurotoxicity · Patient-derived iPSCs

Because our organoids reproduce morphogenesis rather than endpoint identity, they permit quantitative analysis of processes that fail in neural tube defects, the most common congenital malformation of the central nervous system. We have used this system to characterize the transcriptional signature of valproic acid-induced neurulation defects, and to extend such readouts toward teratogenicity screening. In parallel, we model neurodegenerative phenotypes in region-specific organoids carrying disease-linked mutations.

Interpreting these models requires care. Induction efficiency and regional identity vary substantially with the genetic background of the induced pluripotent stem cell line, and such line-to-line variation can be mistaken for a disease phenotype. Characterizing this variability, and establishing reproducible induction as a precondition for phenotypic claims, is a persistent aim of the laboratory.

03

Functional characterization and screening

3D microelectrode arrays · Extracellular electrophysiology · Preclinical screening

Structural fidelity alone is an insufficient criterion for an organoid model. In collaboration with neural engineering groups, we develop three-dimensional high-density microelectrode arrays that record extracellular activity from within intact tissue while permitting local optical stimulation and drug delivery, enabling measurement of synaptic latency and the temporal evolution of network formation.

Current work extends these measurements to parallel recording across organoid arrays. Dose-dependent functional readouts obtained simultaneously from multiple organoids convert sample-to-sample variability into a quantifiable parameter, which is a prerequisite for using patient-derived models in preclinical evaluation.
